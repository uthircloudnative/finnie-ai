# USA Retirement & Tax Rules (Tax Year 2026)

This document contains the official IRS contribution limits, catch-up provisions, and income phase-out ranges for the 2026 tax year.

## 1. 401(k), 403(b), and 457 Plans
These limits apply to employee elective deferrals in workplace retirement plans.
- **Elective Deferral Limit**: **$24,500**
- **Catch-up Contribution (Age 50+)**: **$8,000**
- **"Super" Catch-up (Age 60-63)**: **$11,250** (If plan allows, per SECURE 2.0).
- **Total Combined Plan Limit**: $72,000 (Employee + Employer contributions).

## 2. Traditional and Roth IRAs
The contribution limit applies to the total combined amount across all traditional and Roth IRAs.
- **Standard Contribution Limit**: **$7,500**
- **Catch-up Contribution (Age 50+)**: **$1,100**
- **Total Combined (Age 50+)**: $8,600

## 3. Traditional IRA Deduction Phase-outs (2026)
*If you or your spouse are covered by a workplace plan, your deduction is limited based on Modified AGI:*
- **Single / Head of Household**: $79,000 – $89,000 (Full deduction below $79k, none above $89k).
- **Married Filing Jointly (You are covered)**: $126,000 – $146,000.
- **Married Filing Jointly (You not covered, spouse is)**: $236,000 – $246,000.

## 4. Roth IRA Contribution Phase-outs (2026)
*If your Modified AGI is in this range, your contribution is reduced. Above this, it is $0.*
- **Single / Head of Household**: **$153,000 – $168,000**
- **Married Filing Jointly**: **$242,000 – $252,000**
- **Married Filing Separately**: $0 – $10,000

## 5. Standard Withdrawal Rules
- **Early Access Penalty**: 10% (If accessed before age 59.5, unless exception applies).
- **Required Minimum Distributions (RMD)**: Age 73 (increases to 75 in 2033).
- **Roth IRA 5-Year Rule**: Earnings can only be withdrawn tax-free if the account has been open for 5 years and you are 59.5+.
